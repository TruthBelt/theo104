1 · Approaching the Study of God

  • 2. Faith pp. 9–12
  • 3. Reason/Intellect pp. 15–17

Opens in Logos.

How does a finite mind say anything true about an infinite God? Etzel and Gutierrez name five tools the believer brings to the task: theology, faith, reason, language, and truth.

Everyone Is a Theologian

A theologian is anyone who thinks about God—which is to say, everyone (Etzel and Gutierrez, 3). The question is not whether you hold a view of God, but whether your view is informed, deliberate, and shaped by Scripture. How you see God affects how you treat people, how you steward creation, how you understand your vocation, and how you respond when life is hard.

Faith Is Not Blind

Faith (Greek pistis) means “the conviction that something is true.” Christian faith is not a leap into the dark; it is a trust grounded in evidence—the person of Jesus Christ, His resurrection, and the testimony of His apostles.

  • Faith alone saves (Eph. 2:8–9). It is not earned by works.
  • Faith without works is dead (Jas. 2:17). Genuine faith produces a changed life.
  • Faith is only as good as its object. Faith in Jesus rests on His demonstrated trustworthiness, most decisively His resurrection from the dead (Etzel and Gutierrez, 9–10).

Reason and Intellect Have a Place

Christianity does not require believers to check their brains at the door. Reason is a God-given tool for distinguishing truth from error. The danger is not using reason but elevating it above Scripture. Properly placed, reason serves revelation; it does not overrule it (Etzel and Gutierrez, 15–16).

Language: True, Never Exhaustive

God chose to reveal Himself in known human languages—Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek—so that real understanding is possible. Yet concepts like the Trinity, God’s eternity, and His omniscience strain every analogy we can construct. What God has revealed, we know truly, though never exhaustively (Etzel and Gutierrez, 19–20). This is why theological precision matters: small distortions in language produce large distortions in belief.

Truth: A Fixed Reference Point

Truth is that which corresponds to reality. In a culture that prizes opinion and personal narrative, biblical theology insists that truth exists, that it can be known, and that humanity is accountable to it (Etzel and Gutierrez, 23–25). Truth in Scripture is anchored in a Person and His Word (John 14:6; 17:17).

Theology Proper begins with this conviction: our view of God is the foundation from which we live our lives (Etzel and Small, 40). Everything downstream—sin, salvation, ethics, hope—depends on what we believe about Him.

The Names of God

NameMeaningEmphasis
Elohim“Mighty One”God’s creative power (Gen. 1:1)
Adonai“Master,” “Lord”God’s sovereignty over His kingdom
Yahweh (Jehovah)“I AM,” the self-existent oneGod’s eternal, covenantal personhood (Exod. 3:14)
Jehovah-Rapha“The Lord That Heals”A compound name expressing a specific divine work
Jehovah-Jireh“The Lord Will Provide”God’s gracious provision

Key Attributes Defined

Following Etzel and Small, 43–45:

  • Eternal: God has no beginning and no end.
  • Goodness: God seeks the best for others; His disposition toward His creatures is benevolent.
  • Holy: God is entirely separated from sin; He is pure, blameless, and morally perfect.
  • Justice: God rules His creation rightly and fairly.
  • Love: God seeks the good of others, even at cost to Himself.
  • Mercy: God does not give humanity what its sin deserves.
  • Omnipotence: “All-powerful.” God can do whatever accords with His nature and purposes.
  • Omnipresence: “Present everywhere.” God is present with every place in His whole being.
  • Omniscience: “All-knowing.” God knows everything—past, present, future, and the possible.
  • Spirituality: God is a spirit, not a physical being.
  • Truth: God is without error.
  • Unchanging (immutability): God’s nature never changes, though His actions toward creation may vary.
  • Unity: God’s attributes are inseparable and never at odds with one another.

Jesus as the Fullest Revelation of God

The clearest answer to “What is God like?” is Jesus. He possesses the attributes of God and performs the works of God. To watch Jesus interact with people is to watch God interact with people. Christology and Theology Proper cannot be separated: what you say about Jesus determines what you can say about God (Etzel and Small, 45–46).

Biblical Theism

Against the false views on the slides, biblical theism affirms one personal God who created the world, sustains it, and entered it in the person of Jesus Christ for its redemption (Etzel and Small, 46–47).

3 · The Trinity
ReadEtzel and Small, Everyday Theology

Opens in Logos.

The Classic Formulation

The formula “equal in nature, separate in person, submissive in duties” (Etzel and Small, 52) preserves the biblical balance:

  • Equal in nature: all three persons share the one divine essence. No member of the Trinity is more God than another.
  • Separate in person: each person is distinct; none is the other. They are not three masks worn by one actor.
  • Submissive in duties: in the work of redemption, the Son and the Spirit willingly submit to the Father (Luke 22:42; Matt. 4:1). This submission concerns role, not essence.

Heretical Views

HeresyWhat it denies
TritheismOne nature. It claims three separate gods.
Modalism (Sabellianism)Three persons. It claims one God who appears as Father, then Son, then Spirit, never simultaneously.
ArianismThe Son’s full deity. It claims the Son is the highest created being (see Module 3).

The Athanasian Creed

“And the catholic faith is this: That we worship one God in Trinity, and Trinity in Unity, neither confounding the persons, nor dividing the substance. For there is one Person of the Father, another of the Son, and another of the Holy Spirit. But the godhead of the Father, of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, is all one, the glory equal, the majesty co-eternal.” (Etzel and Small, 54)

The phrases “neither confounding the persons” (don’t merge them—that is modalism) and “nor dividing the substance” (don’t separate them—that is tritheism) summarize centuries of careful theological work.

4 · Creation
ReadEtzel and Small, Everyday Theology
  • 8. Did God Create the World? pp. 57–63
Opens in Logos.

Before God created matter, matter did not exist. God spoke the world into being ex nihilo, “out of nothing” (Etzel and Small, 58). Creation is not a peripheral doctrine; Scripture testifies to it from Genesis to Revelation.

A Spectrum of Beliefs

From most naturalistic to most supernatural (Etzel and Small, 60–61):

ViewClaimCompatible with Scripture?
EvolutionNaturalistic; no supernatural cause. Big bang, natural selection, humanity.No
Deistic EvolutionA divine being started the process, then withdrew.No—denies God’s ongoing immanence
Theistic EvolutionGod initiated and guides evolutionary processes.Disputed; difficult to reconcile with “according to their kinds” (Gen. 1) and a historical Adam
Progressive CreationismGod created directly; the “days” of Genesis 1 represent long ages.Yes—affirms direct creation, debated on “day” length
Fiat CreationismGod created directly by decree in six literal 24-hour days.Yes—historically the majority position

Theological Conclusions

Four conclusions hold regardless of the day-length debate:

  1. Evolutionary explanations of human origins are incompatible with Scripture—the Bible teaches reproduction “according to their kinds” (Gen. 1:11, 24).
  2. God is intimately involved with His creation, not absent from it (Col. 1:17; John 1:14).
  3. Creation is ex nihilo—matter is not eternal.
  4. God created with the appearance of age—Adam and Eve were created as adults, not infants (Gen. 2:22–25).
5 · Transcendence and Immanence
ReadEtzel and Small, Everyday Theology
  • 9. Is God Still Active in the World Today? pp. 65–70
Opens in Logos.

A faithful doctrine of God holds two truths in tension: God is above His world (transcendence) and God is with His world (immanence). Lose either, and the doctrine collapses.

The Transcendence of God

God is entirely separate from and sovereign over His creation. His existence does not depend on the world He made. Transcendence rules out pantheism (which collapses God into creation), materialism (which denies anything beyond creation), and any view that makes God a function of human imagination.

The Immanence of God

Immanence means God is close to and involved with His creation. The God of Scripture is not the absent deity of deism; He acts in history. Psalm 139 captures both truths side by side: God’s omniscience (vv. 1–6), His omnipresence (vv. 7–12), and His omnipotence in forming humanity in the womb (vv. 13–16)—and David’s response is both comfort and conviction.

Is God Still Active Today?

  1. God’s activity is often indirect. God works through His people, His Word, and His Spirit. The visible body of Christ, the church, is one of God’s primary instruments in the world today (Etzel and Small, 70).
  2. God’s activity is sometimes hidden from view. The lack of a visible miracle is not the lack of divine action. God works in ways our finite minds cannot trace (Isa. 55:8–9).

The Word remains “living and active” (Heb. 4:12). The Spirit empowers the church (John 15:26; Acts 1:8). God has not gone silent.

6 · Angels
ReadEtzel and Small, Everyday Theology
  • 10. Are Angels Divine? pp. 71–76
Opens in Logos.

The doctrine of angels matters for two reasons: angels appear throughout Scripture as active participants in God’s plan, and Satan and his demons are fallen angels. Understanding the nature of angels clarifies the nature and limits of the demonic (Etzel and Small, 71–72).

The Nature of Angels

  • Created beings, not divine—created to serve God (Heb. 1:14).
  • Deathless (Luke 20:36).
  • Spirit beings without physical bodies, though they can sometimes appear in physical form (Heb. 13:2).
  • More powerful and intelligent than humans, yet still creatures.

Satan and His Demons

Satan is a created being—once an angel in good standing, now a rebel cast from heaven sometime between Gen. 1:31 (creation declared “very good”) and Gen. 3:1 (his temptation of Eve).

The Believer’s Response

  1. Be ready. Satan tempted Jesus; he will tempt Jesus’ followers (1 Pet. 5:8).
  2. Resist. “Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you” (Jas. 4:7).
  3. Stand firm in the faith. Be “rooted and built up” in Christ (Col. 2:6–7); set your minds on things above (Col. 3:1–2).

Standing firm rests on Christ’s already-accomplished victory at the cross and in the resurrection (1 Cor. 15:57–58).

  2. Someone says, “In the end, God’s love has to win out over His holiness.” What’s the problem with this?

    Show answer

    Answer: God’s attributes never compete. God’s attributes are inseparable and never at odds. His holiness and His love do not compete.

  3. “God made the world like a watchmaker, wound it up, and left it to run on its own.” Which view is this?

    Show answer

    Answer: Deism. Deism grants a creator but denies that He is involved. Biblical theism holds that God sustains the world and entered it in Jesus Christ.

  4. Where has God made Himself known most fully?

    Show answer

    Answer: In Jesus Christ. “The only God, who is at the Father’s side, He has made Him known” (John 1:18). What you say about Jesus determines what you can say about God.

Deck 3: The Trinity

  1. A teacher says God is one person who appeared first as the Father, then as the Son, then as the Spirit. Which error is this?

    Show answer

    Answer: Modalism. Modalism merges the three persons into one. The Athanasian Creed answers it: “neither confounding the persons.”

  2. At Jesus’ baptism (Matt. 3:13–17), what shows that the Father, Son, and Spirit are distinct?

    Show answer

    Answer: All three are present at the same moment. The Son is baptized, the Spirit descends like a dove, and the Father speaks from heaven, all at once.

  3. In redemption, the Son submits to the Father (Luke 22:42). What does that submission mean?

    Show answer

    Answer: It concerns His role, not His being. The persons are equal in nature. Submission in duties is a matter of role in redemption, not a difference in deity.

  4. Why does the egg analogy (shell, white, yolk) fail?

    Show answer

    Answer: The parts differ in nature, but the persons share one nature. Each part of an egg is a different kind of thing. The Father, the Son, and the Spirit share one nature, and each is fully God.

Deck 4: Creation

  1. What does ex nihilo mean?

    Show answer

    Answer: Out of nothing. Before God created matter, matter did not exist. Matter is not eternal, and creation is not made of God.

  2. How do Genesis 1 and Genesis 2 relate?

    Show answer

    Answer: Genesis 1 gives the panoramic overview; Genesis 2 focuses on humanity. The two chapters tell one creation. Genesis 2 looks closely at humanity, the pinnacle of what God made.

  3. Progressive creationists and fiat creationists differ on only one point. Which?

    Show answer

    Answer: The length of the days. Both reject evolution and affirm direct creation. They differ only on how long the days were.

  4. God made Adam and Eve as adults, not as infants. Which conclusion does this illustrate?

    Show answer

    Answer: God created with the appearance of age. Adam and Eve were full-grown on the day they were made. Creation was mature from the start.

Deck 5: Transcendence and Immanence

  1. Someone says, “God is just the universe itself. Everything is divine.” Which truth rebuts this claim?

    Show answer

    Answer: Transcendence. God is above His creation and sovereign over it. Pantheism collapses God into creation.

  2. Which of these is an example of God’s immanence?

    Show answer

    Answer: God walked in the garden. Immanence means God is near and involved. He formed Adam, walked in the garden, spoke to the prophets, and became flesh.

  3. A friend prays, and no visible miracle follows. What does it show?

    Show answer

    Answer: The lack of a visible miracle is not the lack of divine action. God often works indirectly, through His Word, His Spirit, and His church, and sometimes in ways we cannot see.

  4. “Where shall I go from your Spirit? Or where shall I flee from your presence?” (Ps. 139:7). What is David confessing?

    Show answer

    Answer: God is present everywhere. Psalm 139:7–12 confesses God’s omnipresence. The psalm shows God as high above and near at once.

Deck 6: Angels

  1. What does the word angel mean?

    Show answer

    Answer: Messenger. Angels carry God’s word and carry out His decrees, as Gabriel did when he came to Mary (Luke 1:28).

  2. Who is the Angel of the Lord in the Old Testament?

    Show answer

    Answer: The Son appearing before His incarnation. The Angel of the Lord is not a created angel. His appearances are Christophanies, and they occur in the Old Testament only.

  3. Where did Satan come from?

    Show answer

    Answer: God created him good, and he fell. Satan was once an angel in good standing. He was cast out sometime between “very good” (Gen. 1:31) and the temptation (Gen. 3:1).

  4. A believer is told to be ready, to resist, and to stand firm against Satan. What does all of it rest on?

    Show answer

    Answer: Christ’s victory. “Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ” (1 Cor. 15:57).

Why This Matters

Theology Begins with God

Module 1 was about how God reveals Himself; Module 2 is about the God who reveals. The order matters: a low view of God produces a shallow theology of everything downstream—sin, salvation, suffering, ethics, mission.

One God, Three Persons, One Mission

The Trinity is not a riddle to solve; it is the foundation of the gospel. Salvation is the Father sending the Son in the power of the Spirit. Worship is the Spirit drawing believers to the Son for the glory of the Father. Christian community reflects the eternal community of the triune God.

Transcendence and Immanence Together

God is high above and intimately near. Lose His transcendence and you have an idol; lose His immanence and you have a deistic absence. The God of Scripture is the Sovereign King who walked in the garden with Adam, spoke through prophets, and was born in a Bethlehem manger.

A God Who Acts

The same God who created ex nihilo, who orchestrated redemptive history, and who entered creation in Jesus is the God who still works—through His Word, His Spirit, and His Church. The visible quiet is not divine absence.
