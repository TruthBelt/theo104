Module 2 · Essay Guide

Biblical Metanarrative Essay

What the essay asks, how to build it, the mistakes that cost points, and one worked example.

What the Essay Asks

The essay is worth 125 points. More than half of them (78) come from two parts: the four movements and the worldview paragraph.

  1. The four movements, one paragraph each 49 pts

    Explain Creation, Fall, Redemption, and New Creation, each in its own paragraph. Support each movement with at least one Scripture passage, four in all, and show how each movement leads into the next. Bring in at least two theological references from the course materials; your textbooks are enough.

  2. A separate worldview paragraph 29 pts

    Show how the metanarrative helps a person answer the five worldview questions: origin, identity, meaning, morality, and destiny. Write it as one connected paragraph, not a list of answers.

  3. Mechanics and formatting 15 pts

    Proofread. Format in the style of your degree program: APA, MLA, or Turabian. Turabian students use notes-bibliography style (footnotes and a bibliography).

    How do I cite Scripture?

    Each style cites the Bible differently. Find yours.

    APA

    Put the version, year, and verse in parentheses. The verse goes where a page number would.

    (English Standard Bible, 2001/2016, Genesis 1:27)

    Give the full citation again in each new paragraph. Later verses in the same paragraph need only the reference. List the Bible in your references:

    English Standard Bible. (2016). Crossway Bibles. (Original work published 2001)

    Liberty’s APA guide →

    MLA

    First citation: the title, version, and passage, with a period between chapter and verse.

    (The Bible, English Standard Version, Gen. 1.27)

    After that, the passage alone: (Gen. 1.27). List the Bible in your works cited:

    The Bible. English Standard Version, Crossway Bibles, 2016.

    Liberty’s MLA guide →

    Turabian

    Cite the Bible in parentheses, never in footnotes. Name the version at your first citation only. Abbreviate a book’s name only inside the parentheses:

    Christians should not be ashamed of the gospel (Rom. 1:16).

    Leave the Bible out of your bibliography.

    Liberty’s Turabian guide →

  4. Length 12 pts

    750–1,000 words.

  5. A clear position 10 pts

    Take a position and answer every part of the prompt.

  6. Introduction and conclusion 10 pts

    The introduction says why the topic matters and states your thesis. The conclusion brings your points together in support of it.

Use the Biblical Metanarrative Essay Template in Canvas as your guide.

Building It, Paragraph by Paragraph

The instructions call for at least seven paragraphs: an introduction, four movement paragraphs, a worldview paragraph, and a conclusion.

Introduction

Say why the topic matters: the unity of Scripture, a biblical worldview, or both. End with a thesis that previews the steps your essay will take. Write the introduction last, once you know what the body says.

The four movements

Give each movement its own paragraph, and let each paragraph answer a question:

  • Creation: What did God make, and what did He make it for?
  • Fall: What went wrong, and what did it break?
  • Redemption: How does God rescue, and where does the rescue lead? The rescue begins long before the Gospels.
  • New Creation: How does the story end, and how does the ending answer the beginning?

In each paragraph, pick one passage and explain it rather than listing several in parentheses. Go deep, not wide. Show what links each movement to the next, because the Bible tells one story, not four separate episodes. The video Understanding the Metanarrative walks through all four movements.

The worldview paragraph

Keep it separate from the four movements. Draw on all four to answer the questions of origin, identity, meaning, morality, and destiny, and write a paragraph, not a questionnaire. The worked example below shows how.

Conclusion

Bring your points together in support of your thesis. Write the conclusion after the body too. For extra polish, return to why the topic matters.

Common Mistakes

Each of these has cost points on past essays.

In the four movements

Key ideaOne story, not four episodes.
  • Four separate episodes. The Bible tells one story. Show what connects Creation to the Fall, the Fall to Redemption, and Redemption to New Creation.
  • Retelling the story. A summary of what happens in Genesis 3 does not yet explain what the Fall means for the larger story. Explain what each movement does there.
  • A movement named but not explained. Each movement needs a developed paragraph, with its passage unpacked.
  • Scripture listed in parentheses. A string of references counts for little. Choose one passage per movement and explain it.
  • Sources from outside the course. The two theological references must come from the course materials. A website or an outside book does not meet the requirement.

In the worldview paragraph

Key ideaLet the story answer the questions.
  • The questionnaire. Five short answers in a row read like a form. Weave the questions into one discussion.
  • Answers cut off from the story. If the paragraph would read the same without Creation, Fall, Redemption, and New Creation, tie each answer back to them.
  • Skipping questions. Address all five: origin, identity, meaning, morality, and destiny.
  • Folded into the movements. The worldview discussion needs its own paragraph.

Everywhere else

  • A topic without a thesis. Naming the subject in your introduction is not the same as stating what you will argue.
  • Dropped quotations. Introduce every quotation with its source.
  • Lowercase sacred terms. Capitalize Bible, Scripture, Old Testament, New Testament, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

Worked Example: Tracing One Question

The worldview paragraph works when each answer grows out of the story. Here is the method, applied to one of the five questions. The other four are yours.

The question · IdentityWhat does it mean to be human?

Ask what each movement says about it.

  • Creation“God created man in his own image” (Gen. 1:27).Note: our dignity is given by God, not earned.
  • Fall“All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God” (Rom. 3:23).Note: every image-bearer has sinned.
  • RedemptionIn Christ, the believer’s new self “is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator” (Col. 3:10).Note: God is renewing His people after His image.
  • New Creation“We shall be like him, because we shall see him as he is” (1 John 3:2).Note: what God began at creation, He will finish.

Now do the same for origin, meaning, morality, and destiny.

Then turn your notes into one connected paragraph, weaving the questions together rather than answering them one by one.
