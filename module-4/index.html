Two Views

Continuationism

All the gifts continue until Christ returns. The church waits “not lacking in any gift” for His revealing (1 Cor. 1:7), and prophecy and tongues pass away “when the perfect comes” (1 Cor. 13:8–10), read as His return. Views within it differ on how central the sign gifts are.

Cessationism

The miraculous gifts ended with the apostolic era. Given directly and complete, they belonged to the church’s foundation (Eph. 2:20) as the apostles’ credentials (2 Cor. 12:12). Hebrews already speaks of that witness in the past tense (Heb. 2:3–4). God still heals as He wills, and the ordinary gifts continue.

1 · Who Is the Holy Spirit?
ReadEtzel and Small, Everyday Theology
  • 16. Is the Holy Spirit Really God? pp. 115–120
Opens in Logos.

Pneumatology (from the Greek pneuma, “spirit” or “breath”) is the branch of systematic theology that studies the person and work of the Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity. Module 3 asked who Jesus is and what He has done. This module asks the parallel question of the Spirit: Who is the Holy Spirit, and what does He do? Of the three persons, He is the one most often overlooked, yet the questions about Him are no less important than any in Christian doctrine (Etzel and Small, 115).

Fully God

The deity of the Holy Spirit is the doctrine that the Spirit is fully God: eternal, uncreated, sharing the one divine nature with the Father and the Son (Etzel and Small, 115). Scripture calls Him God. Peter told Ananias that in lying “to the Holy Spirit” he had “not lied to man but to God” (Acts 5:3–4). The case also rests on three further lines of evidence.

  • His works. He was “hovering over the face of the waters” at creation (Gen. 1:2; Ps. 104:30); He “rushed upon David” when Samuel anointed him (1 Sam. 16:13); and Jesus, “full of the Holy Spirit,” was “led by the Spirit in the wilderness” (Luke 4:1). Jesus said His own departure was to the disciples’ advantage, because then the Helper would come (John 16:7), and He named the Spirit with the Father and the Son in the one name of baptism (Matt. 28:19; Etzel and Small, 116–17).
  • His attributes. He is omnipresent (Ps. 139:7–10), omniscient, searching “even the depths of God” (1 Cor. 2:10–11), and omnipotent (Luke 1:35; Etzel and Small, 117).
  • The church’s confession. The Apostles’ Creed confesses “the Holy Spirit” alongside the Father and the Son. The Nicene Creed calls Him “the Lord, the giver of life,” who “with the Father and the Son is worshiped and glorified.” The Athanasian Creed: “The Father is uncreated, the Son is uncreated, the Holy Spirit is uncreated” (Etzel and Small, 118). To be worshiped and glorified with the Father and the Son is to be God.

A Person, Not a Force

“The Holy Spirit is a person, not a vague force” (Erickson, quoted in Etzel and Small, 120). Jesus called Him “another Helper” (John 14:16), a title for a personal helper, not an energy. He teaches (John 14:26), bears witness about Jesus (John 15:26), guides and speaks (John 16:13), intercedes (Rom. 8:26–27), distributes gifts as He wills (1 Cor. 12:11), and can be grieved (Eph. 4:30). Those are the acts of a person.

His personhood is essential to the Trinity: not “the Father, the Son, and a divine power,” but three persons who share one nature, “equal in nature, separate in person, and submissive in duties” (Etzel and Small, 51–53). Each person is distinct, and none is the other; the Spirit’s submission belongs to the work of redemption, in which He is sent and serves. It is a matter of role, not being.

He Points to Jesus

In humility the Spirit points away from Himself to Christ: “He will glorify Me, for He will take what is Mine and declare it to you” (John 16:14; Etzel and Small, 119). The selflessness that makes Him easy to overlook is itself a mark of His personhood.

2 · Then and Now
ReadEtzel and Gutierrez, Theology Applied
  • 34. Indwelling/Filling of the Holy Spirit pp. 189–191
Opens in Logos.

The Spirit has always been at work, but His ministry takes a decisive new shape after Pentecost. Seeing the difference guards against two errors: imagining He was absent before Pentecost, or flattening the new gift believers now have.

Before Pentecost

The Spirit often came upon chosen people for a chosen work. He filled craftsmen to build the tabernacle (Exod. 31:3; 35:31), rushed upon David as king (1 Sam. 16:13), and anointed the prophets: “The Spirit of the Lord God is upon Me” (Isa. 61:1; Etzel and Gutierrez, 189). That presence could be withdrawn. “The Spirit of the Lord departed from Saul” (1 Sam. 16:14), and David, having watched it, prayed, “Take not Your Holy Spirit from me” (Ps. 51:11), a plea about his anointing as king, not his salvation. Etzel and Gutierrez speak of the Spirit’s indwelling and filling in both Testaments (Etzel and Gutierrez, 189); what Scripture does not show before Pentecost is His permanent dwelling in every believer.

The Spirit also regenerated believers before Pentecost. No one enters the kingdom without the new birth (John 3:3), and Jesus expected Israel’s teacher to know it (John 3:10); the Law spoke of it as a circumcised heart (Deut. 30:6). The Spirit gave new life in every age and abode with those He saved. Through Ezekiel God promised Israel a day when He would put His Spirit within them (Ezek. 36:26–27).

Since Pentecost

Jesus told the disciples that the Spirit “dwells with you and will be in you” (John 14:17). He “had not been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified” (John 7:39). Then the exalted Christ “poured out” the Spirit (Acts 2:33), and He now dwells in every believer, permanently.

3 · Saved by the Spirit
ReadEtzel and Small, Everyday Theology
  • 17. What Is the Role of the Holy Spirit in Salvation? pp. 121–125
Opens in Logos.

At the moment of salvation the Holy Spirit does four things at once: regeneration, baptism, indwelling, and sealing (Etzel and Small, 121). How a person answers “What does the Spirit do in salvation?” shapes everything else. If we save ourselves, we must keep ourselves saved by our works; but if salvation is God’s work, God who began it will complete it. “From the start, salvation is a work of God” (Etzel and Small, 124).

Regeneration: New Birth

Regeneration is new birth, a spiritual re-creation. “Unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God” (John 3:3). It is the Spirit’s doing: “He saved us … by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit” (Titus 3:5). “If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation” (2 Cor. 5:17); we cannot make ourselves new (Etzel and Small, 122).

Before the new birth, the Spirit convicts “the world concerning sin and righteousness and judgment” (John 16:8). He convicts where the Word is heard, and conviction can be resisted (Acts 7:51). It is not the new birth itself.

Spirit Baptism: Into One Body

Spirit baptism is not water baptism. It is Christ’s act of placing the believer, by means of the Spirit, into the body of Christ: “In one Spirit we were all baptized into one body” (1 Cor. 12:13). John called Jesus the one “who baptizes with the Holy Spirit” (John 1:33; Matt. 3:11). It happens once, at faith, to every believer since Pentecost, and it marks the believer as a member of God’s family, sharing one identity and mission with all other Christians. Scripture never commands believers to be baptized by the Spirit; it is not a second blessing to seek.

Indwelling: God Takes Up Residence

At salvation the Spirit comes to live within the believer: “Your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you” (1 Cor. 6:19). The church together is God’s temple too (1 Cor. 3:16). As God dwelt in the temple, He now dwells in His people (Etzel and Small, 123). Having the Spirit marks a believer: “Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him” (Rom. 8:9). This indwelling makes possible the abiding life Jesus described: “Abide in Me, and I in you” (John 15:4).

Sealing: God Guarantees the Outcome

The indwelling Spirit is Himself God’s seal on the believer and the guarantee of the inheritance (Eph. 1:13–14; 2 Cor. 1:22). Like a down payment on a house, He is God’s pledge that the full purchase will be completed (Etzel and Small, 124). “He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ” (Phil. 1:6). Scripture never commands believers to be sealed, only not to grieve the Spirit by whom they were sealed (Eph. 4:30).

Salvation is the beginning. The same Spirit who saves goes on working to make the believer like Jesus, who are predestined “to be conformed to the image of His Son” (Rom. 8:29; Etzel and Small, 127).

Filling: Under His Control

To be filled with the Spirit is to be under His control. “Do not get drunk with wine, for that is debauchery, but be filled with the Spirit” (Eph. 5:18). As wine governs a drunk man, the Spirit is to govern the believer (Etzel and Gutierrez, 189). Filling differs from baptism and indwelling, which happen once to every believer and cannot be lost. Filling is commanded, continual, and a matter of degree: the believer does not get more of the Spirit; the Spirit takes fuller control of him. It is not a crisis experience to seek. The believer is filled as he yields to the Spirit and obeys the Word, and its marks follow in the same passage: singing to one another, thanksgiving, and submitting to one another (Eph. 5:19–21). Sin grieves the Spirit (Eph. 4:30), and believers can quench Him (1 Thess. 5:19).

Sanctification: Set Apart

Sanctification means being set apart: away from sin, and to God. “You shall be holy, for I am holy” (1 Pet. 1:16), a holiness only God can produce in us, chiefly through the Spirit. The textbook describes it in three tenses (Etzel and Small, 128–29):

TenseAlso calledWhat it means
PastPositionalSet apart to God at the moment of salvation
PresentProgressiveBeing made more like Christ throughout life
FutureProspectiveFully set apart from sin in our heavenly home

Illumination: Light on the Word

The unbelieving mind is blinded (2 Cor. 4:4), and “the natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God” (1 Cor. 2:14). Illumination is the Spirit’s work on the reader, enabling him to receive the Word, judge it rightly, and obey it: “Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of Your law” (Ps. 119:18). It adds nothing to Scripture, and it does not replace study. The Bereans “received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily” (Acts 17:11); study and the Spirit work together (Etzel and Gutierrez, 49).

Prayer and Renewal

When we do not know how to pray, “the Spirit Himself intercedes for us with groanings too deep for words … according to the will of God” (Rom. 8:26–27). Closely related is renewal, the ongoing renovation of the believer’s mind: “Be transformed by the renewal of your mind” (Rom. 12:2). Regeneration happens once; renewal is “a day-by-day constant renovation” (Etzel and Gutierrez, 185–86).

Holding Back Evil

Beyond His work in believers, the Spirit restrains sin in the world, so that people are not as evil as they could be, without saving all whom He restrains. He works directly on the conscience (Rom. 2:15) and through means (Etzel and Small, 130–31):

  1. The individual Christian, who resists sin and influences those nearby.
  2. The church, a congregation equipped for ministry (Eph. 4:11–16) that restrains evil in its community.
  3. Human institutions: families, schools, and governments. Laws that protect human life are one way the Spirit restrains evil through the governing authorities (Rom. 13:4).

This ministry connects to the restrainer who holds back lawlessness until the appointed time (2 Thess. 2:6–7). The restrainer is the Holy Spirit, who restrains mostly through believers and through government. When He is “out of the way,” He stops restraining, but He does not leave the earth. 5 · Gifts of the Spirit
ReadEtzel and Small, Everyday Theology
  • 19. What Spiritual Gifts Are Available Today? pp. 133–139
Opens in Logos.

A spiritual gift (Greek charisma) is a gift given by the Holy Spirit to a Christian for serving the church (Etzel and Small, 134). “To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good” (1 Cor. 12:7). Five passages list about twenty gifts (Rom. 12:3–8; 1 Cor. 12:4–11; 12:27–30; Eph. 4:11; 1 Pet. 4:10–11).

Three Truths About the Gifts

  1. The Spirit is the source. He gives “to each one individually as He wills” (1 Cor. 12:11). Every believer has at least one gift and no one has them all (1 Cor. 12:21). A gift is more than a natural talent, though the Spirit may take one up. Gifts are received, not sought.
  2. The gifts are for others. “As each has received a gift, use it to serve one another” (1 Pet. 4:10). “If you have a gift, the gift is not for you” (Etzel and Small, 138). Scripture commands believers to use their gifts, not to discover them.
  3. The gifts are for unity. Every list sits inside an appeal for humility and unity (Etzel and Small, 136–38). A gift is no measure of spirituality: Corinth lacked no gift (1 Cor. 1:7), yet Paul called its members “infants in Christ” (1 Cor. 3:1).

The Debate Over Sign Gifts

The most heated debate concerns the sign gifts (prophecy, healing, tongues) as distinct from service gifts (teaching, giving, showing mercy; Etzel and Small, 138). Christians answer in two main ways:

PositionClaim
ContinuationismAll the gifts continue until Christ returns. The church waits “not lacking in any gift” for His revealing (1 Cor. 1:7), and prophecy and tongues pass away “when the perfect comes” (1 Cor. 13:8–10), read as His return. Views within it differ on how central the sign gifts are.
CessationismThe miraculous gifts ended with the apostolic era. Given directly and complete, they belonged to the church’s foundation (Eph. 2:20) as the apostles’ credentials (2 Cor. 12:12), and Hebrews already speaks of that witness in the past tense (Heb. 2:3–4). God still heals as He wills, and the ordinary gifts continue.

Wherever a Christian lands, the New Testament’s priority is plain: “strive to excel in building up the church” (1 Cor. 14:12). Gifts serve unity, humility, and the glory of God, never division (Etzel and Small, 139).

The fruit of the Spirit is the Christlike character that grows in a believer who walks in obedience to the Holy Spirit (Gal. 5:22–23).

Gifts and Fruit

GiftsFruit
PurposeServe the churchShow Christlike character

No Christian has every gift, but every Christian is to show all the fruit (Etzel and Small, 142). “Fruit” is singular, and it grows as a unit: no one grows in love while neglecting joy (Etzel and Small, 145). Paul sets the list against the works of the flesh (Gal. 5:19–21) and frames it with a command: “Walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh” (Gal. 5:16, 25).

The Nine Facets

FacetSense
LoveSelf-giving pursuit of another’s good (1 John 4:8)
JoyGladness that does not depend on circumstances (Heb. 12:2)
PeaceWholeness in a right relationship with Christ (John 14:27)
PatienceSlowness to anger, and endurance (Ps. 145:8)
KindnessGoodness in action, generous toward others (Titus 3:4)
GoodnessMoral uprightness and usefulness (Ps. 136:1)
FaithfulnessSteady reliability toward God and others, reflecting God’s own (Lam. 3:23)
GentlenessStrength under control; meekness, not weakness (2 Cor. 10:1)
Self-controlRestraint of sinful desire by the Spirit’s power (Titus 2:12)

A Matter of the Heart

The fruit is not behavior modification. “The fruit of the Spirit is only evidenced after there has been a change of one’s heart” (Etzel and Small, 144). When the Spirit changes what a person loves, changed desires follow, and changed behavior flows from them. The real question is, Do we love God more than we love sin? (Etzel and Small, 145).

7 · Mission and Worship
ReadEtzel and Gutierrez, Theology Applied
  • 31. The Great Commandment pp. 177–179
  • 32. The Great Commission pp. 181–183
  • 35. Life on Mission pp. 193–195
Opens in Logos.

The Spirit’s indwelling and filling are not ends in themselves; they turn the believer outward, into love, mission, and worship.

The Great Commandment

Asked for the greatest commandment, Jesus answered, “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind.… You shall love your neighbor as yourself” (Matt. 22:37, 39). The whole Law is summed up in two charges: love God and love others. The verb is agapaō, a self-giving love (Etzel and Gutierrez, 177). The world will know Jesus’ followers by it (John 13:35), and it comes first in the fruit the Spirit produces (Gal. 5:22).

The Great Commission

Jesus gave His followers their commission more than once (Matt. 28:18–20; Luke 24:46–48; John 20:21; Acts 1:8). Its center is “make disciples” (Greek mathēteuō), which includes teaching (Etzel and Gutierrez, 181). The Spirit empowers it: “You will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth” (Acts 1:8). The witness moves outward from Jerusalem to the whole world (Etzel and Gutierrez, 182).

Life on Mission

A believer controlled by the Spirit lives on mission rather than drifting: “The love of Christ controls us … that those who live might no longer live for themselves” (2 Cor. 5:14–15). Paul could say, “Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ” (1 Cor. 11:1), and Jesus lived toward the hour for which He came: “Father, the hour has come” (John 17:1; Etzel and Gutierrez, 193–94).

Worship in the Spirit

Where the Spirit fills a congregation, the marks of Eph. 5:19–21 appear in its worship:

  • Sung praise: “singing and making melody to the Lord with your heart.”
  • Thanksgiving: “giving thanks always and for everything to God the Father.”
  • Mutual submission: “submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ.”
  • Christ at the center: because the Spirit glorifies the Son (John 16:14), Spirit-filled worship points to Jesus, never to itself. It is also honest and personal: no one must copy another’s outward expression to be spiritual (Etzel and Gutierrez, 79).

Why This Matters

The Spirit Is Fully God and Fully Personal

To shortchange either truth distorts the Trinity. Deny the Spirit’s deity and He becomes a lesser god; deny His personhood and He becomes a mere force. Scripture and the creeds confess Him as the third person of the one God, worthy of the same honor as the Father and the Son.

The Spirit Makes Salvation God’s Work from Start to Finish

Regeneration, baptism, indwelling, and sealing all happen to us, not by us. The believer’s assurance rests not on personal strength but on the God who seals His people and pledges to complete what He began.

The Spirit Produces Christ, Not Merely Power

The Spirit’s aim is not spectacular experience but Christlikeness: the fruit of love, joy, and peace, and a life set apart to God. Gifts serve the body; fruit reveals the heart. The Christian who chases the one while neglecting the other has missed the Spirit’s purpose.

The Spirit Points to Jesus

In humility the Spirit glorifies the Son. He testifies to Christ in salvation, conforms the believer to Christ in sanctification, and centers the church’s worship and mission on Christ. To walk in the Spirit is, finally, to be drawn ever closer to Jesus.

Check Yourself

Four questions for each deck. Choose an answer to check it. Nothing is saved.

Deck 1: Who Is the Holy Spirit?

  1. What does pneumatology study?

    Show answer

    Answer: The person and work of the Holy Spirit. From the Greek pneuma, “spirit” or “breath”: the branch of theology that studies who the Holy Spirit is and what He does.

  2. What does Peter’s rebuke of Ananias show about the Holy Spirit (Acts 5:3–4)?

    Show answer

    Answer: To lie to the Holy Spirit is to lie to God. Peter said Ananias had lied “to the Holy Spirit,” then said he had “not lied to man but to God.” Scripture calls the Spirit God.

  3. Which of these shows that the Holy Spirit is a person, not a force?

    Show answer

    Answer: He teaches, intercedes, and can be grieved. Teaching, interceding, and being grieved are acts of a person (John 14:26; Rom. 8:26; Eph. 4:30).

  4. Why did Jesus say it was to the disciples’ advantage that He go away (John 16:7)?

    Show answer

    Answer: Then the Helper would come to them. Jesus counted the Spirit’s coming a greater gain than His own bodily presence. No creature could be that.

Deck 2: Then and Now

  1. Before Pentecost, how did the Spirit most often work through the people He empowered?

    Show answer

    Answer: He came upon chosen people for a particular work. He filled craftsmen, rushed upon kings, and anointed prophets for the work God gave them (Exod. 31:3; 1 Sam. 16:13; Isa. 61:1).

  2. What does 1 Sam. 16:14 record about King Saul?

    Show answer

    Answer: The Spirit of the Lord departed from him. The Spirit’s coming upon a leader could be withdrawn. Having seen it happen to Saul, David prayed, “Take not Your Holy Spirit from me” (Ps. 51:11).

  3. What did God promise Israel through Ezekiel?

    Show answer

    Answer: A new heart, and His Spirit put within them. “I will give you a new heart … And I will put My Spirit within you” (Ezek. 36:26–27).

  4. In John 7:39, why had the Spirit “not been given” yet?

    Show answer

    Answer: Jesus was not yet glorified. Once exalted at the right hand of God, Christ “poured out” the Spirit (Acts 2:33), who now dwells in every believer, as Jesus had promised: “He dwells with you and will be in you” (John 14:17).

Deck 3: Saved by the Spirit

  1. According to Titus 3:5, why did God save us?

    Show answer

    Answer: According to His own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit. “He saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to His own mercy.” Salvation is God’s work from start to finish.

  2. How does the Spirit’s conviction (John 16:8) differ from the new birth?

    Show answer

    Answer: It can be resisted, and it is not the new birth itself. The Spirit convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment through the Word, but a person can resist Him (Acts 7:51). New birth is His giving life.

  3. Which statement about Spirit baptism is true?

    Show answer

    Answer: Christ places every believer into one body by the Spirit, once, at faith. “In one Spirit we were all baptized into one body” (1 Cor. 12:13). Jesus is the one “who baptizes with the Holy Spirit” (John 1:33), and Scripture never commands believers to seek it.

  4. What does Paul call the Spirit with whom believers are sealed (Eph. 1:13–14)?

    Show answer

    Answer: The guarantee of our inheritance. Like a down payment, the Spirit is God’s pledge that He will finish the salvation He began (Phil. 1:6).

Deck 4: Life in the Spirit

  1. Which describes progressive, or present, sanctification?

    Show answer

    Answer: Being made more like Christ throughout life. Sanctification has three tenses: positional (past), progressive (present), and prospective (future).

  2. Which statement about the Spirit’s illumination is true?

    Show answer

    Answer: It adds nothing to Scripture and does not replace study. The Spirit enables the reader to receive the Word, judge it rightly, and obey it. The Bereans still examined “the Scriptures daily” (Acts 17:11).

  3. How does renewal differ from regeneration?

    Show answer

    Answer: Regeneration happens once; renewal goes on day by day. Regeneration is new birth. Renewal is “a day-by-day constant renovation”: “be transformed by the renewal of your mind” (Rom. 12:2).

  4. Through what means does the Spirit restrain sin in the world?

    Show answer

    Answer: Through believers, the church, and institutions such as the family and government. He also works directly on the conscience (Rom. 2:15). Laws that protect human life are one way He restrains evil (Rom. 13:4).

Deck 5: Gifts of the Spirit

  1. According to 1 Cor. 12:7, what are spiritual gifts for?

    Show answer

    Answer: The common good of the church. “To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good.” If you have a gift, it is not for you.

  2. Who decides which gifts a believer receives?

    Show answer

    Answer: The Spirit, as He wills. The Spirit “apportions to each one individually as He wills” (1 Cor. 12:11). Gifts are received, not sought.

  3. What does the church at Corinth show about spiritual gifts?

    Show answer

    Answer: A gift is no measure of spirituality. Corinth was “not lacking in any gift” (1 Cor. 1:7), yet Paul called its members “infants in Christ” (1 Cor. 3:1).

  4. Whatever view of the sign gifts a Christian holds, what does 1 Cor. 14:12 make the priority?

    Show answer

    Answer: Building up the church. “Strive to excel in building up the church.” Gifts serve unity, humility, and the glory of God.

Deck 6: Fruit of the Spirit

  1. Why does Paul write “fruit,” singular, rather than “fruits”?

    Show answer

    Answer: The nine qualities grow together, as one. The fruit grows as a unit: no one grows in love while neglecting joy.

  2. What does Paul set against the fruit of the Spirit in Galatians 5?

    Show answer

    Answer: The works of the flesh. The works of the flesh (Gal. 5:19–21) stand against the fruit, and Paul calls believers to “walk by the Spirit” (Gal. 5:16).

  3. Which best describes the facet of faithfulness?

    Show answer

    Answer: Steady reliability toward God and others. It reflects God’s own faithfulness: “Great is Your faithfulness” (Lam. 3:23). The other choices describe gentleness, joy, and patience.

  4. Where does the fruit of the Spirit come from?

    Show answer

    Answer: A changed heart, which changes desires, which changes behavior. “The fruit of the Spirit is only evidenced after there has been a change of one’s heart” (Etzel and Small, p. 144).

Deck 7: Mission and Worship

  1. How did Jesus sum up the whole Law in the Great Commandment?

    Show answer

    Answer: Love God and love others. “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart … You shall love your neighbor as yourself” (Matt. 22:37, 39).

  2. What is the center of the Great Commission?

    Show answer

    Answer: Making disciples, which includes teaching. “Go therefore and make disciples of all nations … teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you” (Matt. 28:19–20).

  3. According to 2 Cor. 5:14–15, what does Christ’s love do in those who live by His death?

    Show answer

    Answer: It moves them to live no longer for themselves but for Him. “The love of Christ controls us … that those who live might no longer live for themselves but for Him.”

  4. Which is a mark of worship where the Spirit fills a congregation?

    Show answer

    Answer: Christ at the center. The Spirit glorifies the Son (John 16:14), so Spirit-filled worship points to Jesus. It is also honest: no one must copy another’s outward expression to be spiritual.
